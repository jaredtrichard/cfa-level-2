01Foundation

An investment question comes first.

An equity analyst notices that companies with faster sales growth often have higher profit margins. That observation is interesting, but it is not enough to build an investment forecast. Those companies may also differ in scale, input costs, or business mix. A relationship seen in a simple comparison may change when other relevant differences are considered.

Multiple linear regression relates one outcome to several explanatory variables. The outcome is the dependent variable, often called the response or Y. The explanatory inputs are the independent variables, also called predictors or X variables. “Independent” names their role in the model; it does not promise that the predictors are unrelated to one another. [2]

In the analyst’s problem, profit margin could be Y, with sales growth, company size, and input-cost changes as predictors. Each observation would contain the margin and the corresponding input values for a clearly defined company and period. Before estimating anything, the analyst must decide whether the goal is to understand differences in margins or predict a future margin.

What “multiple” refers to

There are several predictors for one outcome. A long list of observations does not, by itself, make a regression “multiple.”

This outcome is about choosing a sensible use for regression and describing the work from question to decision. Formal equations, individual coefficient interpretation, and detailed tests receive their own modules.

02Investment uses

Explanation and prediction answer different questions.

Regression can support an analyst’s understanding of investment relationships or a prediction task. The same broad technique serves both purposes, but the objective changes what must be specified and how results should be evaluated. Software estimates a chosen model; the analyst remains responsible for choosing the question and interpreting the answer. [1]

Understand the drivers of returns

A portfolio manager asks why a bond strategy moves when government yields and credit spreads change. The response can be the strategy’s return over each observed period. Yield changes and spread changes are candidate predictors for those same periods. Studying them jointly helps describe which market exposures appear in the strategy’s behavior.

This is useful for a portfolio decision: holdings that look different by name may respond to similar market forces. A manager can use the regression analysis as evidence when reviewing concentrations. It is an input to portfolio construction, not a complete portfolio optimizer or a guarantee that historical exposures will persist.

Estimate a future operating result

An equity analyst wants a forecast of a retailer’s next-quarter operating profit. Candidate inputs might include information already available about store traffic, wage costs, and contracted rents. Here, success means useful predictions on data not used to select or estimate the model. A variable can be economically plausible yet add little useful forecast information.

Compare securities with different characteristics

A credit analyst asks whether a bond’s spread looks unusual relative to comparable bonds after allowing for maturity, leverage, and liquidity. The response is the spread; the predictors describe relevant differences among the bonds at the observation date. A gap between observed and model-implied spread is a reason to investigate further, not automatic proof of mispricing. A missing covenant or unusual risk could explain it.

Describe the problem concretely.

Name the investment decision, the response, plausible predictors, and whether the purpose is explanation or prediction. “Use regression to analyze investments” does not identify an actual task.

03Boundaries

Useful relationships are not automatic causes.

A model may associate a company’s profitability with its spending on research. That does not, by itself, show what would happen if management increased that spending. Profitable companies may have more cash to invest; an omitted characteristic may affect both variables; the timing may run in the opposite direction from the story the analyst tells.

Adding controls can make a comparison more informative, but it does not automatically establish a causal effect. Evidence about a statistical relationship and evidence about an intervention are different claims. Be precise about which claim the study supports.

Prediction has a separate limitation. A model cannot use an input in a live forecast unless that input is known then or is itself forecast. Explaining a completed quarter with that quarter’s realized industry results is different from predicting the quarter before those results exist.

Model purpose and interpretation: [3]. Predictive information availability: [5].

Check that the response suits the method

Returns, margins, spreads, and profits are numerical responses that can motivate a linear regression analysis. A yes/no response, such as default versus no default, raises a different modeling problem. Later curriculum material introduces logistic regression for that setting. Do not choose ordinary multiple linear regression simply because many inputs are available.

“Linear” concerns how the model combines its estimated parameters. This lesson does not require constructing transformed models. It requires recognizing that choosing the model’s form is part of the analyst’s work. [4]

04Workflow

Move from a question to a defensible use.

The regression process is iterative: a poor diagnostic result may send the analyst back to the data or specification. The sequence below is a practical synthesis, not a set of CFA-prescribed labels to memorize. Its essential stages are formulation, estimation, evaluation, and use. [7]

Define the question and specify the model

Identify Y, candidate X variables, the purpose, observation unit, measurement period, and proposed form. Use investment reasoning to justify the inputs. “Predict operating profit next quarter” is more precise than “explain company performance.” [1]

Collect and inspect the data

Check missing values, unusual observations, consistent units, and measurement definitions. A spreadsheet can contain valid numbers that describe incompatible periods. Explore relationships before treating the dataset as ready for estimation. [3]

Estimate the candidate model

Software estimates the unknown coefficients—the numerical weights and baseline in the model. Ordinary least squares chooses coefficients to minimize the sum of squared residuals on the fitting data. A residual is the observed response minus its fitted value. Squaring prevents positive and negative deviations from canceling and places greater weight on large deviations. [4]

Evaluate and revise

Consider economic plausibility, fit, uncertainty, and whether model assumptions are reasonable. Examine residuals and influential observations. A successful software run is not a validity check. If problems appear, investigate their source before changing or rejecting the specification. [3]

Validate, interpret, and use

For prediction, evaluate observations that did not guide model selection. For explanation, give particular attention to whether the specification and evidence support the relationship being discussed. Communicate limits, then revisit the model when new data or a changed economic setting undermine its usefulness. [3]

05Worked case

Design a retailer’s profit forecast.

North Quay Retail is a fictional listed retailer. An analyst wants to forecast next-quarter operating profit before publishing an equity valuation. This example follows the whole process without assuming that regression software can make the investment decisions for the analyst.

Make the objective observable

The response is next-quarter operating profit, measured in millions of dollars. Each observation is a historical forecast date paired with the profit subsequently reported for its target quarter. Candidate predictors include store-traffic information available by that date and wage-cost changes already announced. Contracted rents may be another candidate, subject to data quality and relevance.

The forecast horizon determines the dataset. Using the target quarter’s final sales as an input would answer a different question if final sales are unavailable when the forecast must be made. Calling that number a “predictor” does not make it available earlier.

Make the data comparable

The analyst checks how traffic was measured, whether accounting definitions changed, and whether acquired stores altered the business represented by the sample. An unusual quarter may be an error, a one-off event, or useful evidence about risk. It should be investigated rather than deleted merely because it hurts fit.

Fit on earlier observations; assess later ones

Suppose the analyst estimates a compact candidate model and a larger candidate model on earlier data. Later quarters are reserved to test forecasting. A forecast evaluation should respect time order; randomly mixing past and future can create an unrealistic test for time-dependent observations. [6]

Interpret the result, then return to the objective

The compact model makes smaller squared forecast errors in this illustration. Even if the larger model fitted the earlier sample better, that would not overturn what happened in these later quarters. The analyst should still inspect the size and pattern of misses, sample relevance, and investment usefulness before selecting a forecast model.

If the compact forecast becomes an input to the valuation, the analyst should explain the uncertainty around that input. The regression’s job is to inform the forecast; it does not independently validate the entire equity valuation.

06Quality control

Recognize the shortcuts that break the process.

Searching first and inventing a story afterward

If the retailer analyst tries every available column and only afterward decides what the model means, an attractive result can reflect accidental patterns. The research question and economic reasoning should guide candidate selection. Searching is not a substitute for a rationale.

Letting future information enter the past

Suppose a supplier releases a revised cost series after the target quarter ends. A historical prediction built using that revision may look better than a prediction possible at the time. Data leakage can also arise when feature selection or fitted preprocessing uses the eventual test observations. Keep model-development information separate from the final evaluation. [5]

Treating fit as the only criterion

A model that describes the fitting sample closely can still forecast poorly or support an unreliable explanation. More inputs are not automatically better. The retailer comparison makes that distinction visible: the desired outcome was useful later-quarter forecasts, not a flattering description of the estimation period.

Promoting an unexplained difference into a discovery

For the bond analyst, an unusually large spread relative to model estimates might indicate an opportunity—or an omitted risk. For the portfolio manager, unexplained return is not automatically investment skill. In each case, the correct next step is investigation grounded in the investment question.

Know where the next modules begin.

Here you need to describe why residual checks, assumptions, and statistical evaluation matter. Computing tests, interpreting individual regression coefficients, and diagnosing specific residual patterns are separate learning outcomes.

07Recall

Describe a complete regression study.

A strong explanation starts with the investment problem, identifies what is being explained or predicted, and names the information used. It then describes specifying the model, preparing the data, estimating it, checking its adequacy, and using the results within their limits. If evaluation reveals a problem, explain why the analyst may have to return to an earlier stage.

For this checkbox, the numerical example is there to make evaluation concrete. The required action is to describe the investment uses and process; deriving an estimator or hand-calculating regression coefficients is not a prerequisite for answering it.

The central distinction

Regression estimation produces a fitted model. Regression analysis determines whether that model provides a useful, defensible answer to the investment question.
